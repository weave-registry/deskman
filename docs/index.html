Three parts that work together

An assistant for the requests you can say in a sentence, a grid for the ones you do a hundred times a day, and a memory so you do fewer of either.

💬

An assistant

Type a request in the floating panel (⌃⌥Space). The on-device model picks a tool, and Deskman makes the move. "This window" means the one you were using: the panel never takes focus.

▦

A grid

Five snap zones. Smart Arrange fills them for you, or you fill them with ⌃⌘1…5 or by dragging a window onto one. Deskman learns your own grid from your moves and keeps its zones equal.

🧠

A memory

Deskman learns how you arrange your desk and offers it back. It names each window by what it is for, and keeps the windows of one project together.

✨

Smart Arrange

Your browser goes in the middle and the windows you use most go next to it. A window that is already in a zone stays there. No window ever gets smaller than 700 × 400 pt, and every arrange can be undone.

🔁

Keep Arranged

Turn it on and Smart Arrange runs every time your windows change. It waits until things settle, and it holds off for as long as you are working in a window.

🗺️

The desktop map

See every window, what Smart Arrange would do, and what Deskman has learned, in one picture. The decision log says why it did each thing.

Expand on hover

Two windows stacked in a column each get half the height, which is often too little to read. Rest the mouse on one and it gets the room.

  • At least three quarters of the column goes to the window under the mouse.
  • Nothing is hidden. The windows above and below shrink to strips of at least 120 pt and stay in place.
  • Rest on a strip and the room moves to that window instead.
  • Move away and it snaps back, unless you moved or resized a window meanwhile. A window you touched stays where you put it.
  • It is a hover, not a habit. It pins nothing and teaches the learner nothing. It never runs during a drag or a meeting, or for apps you told Deskman to leave alone.

On its own, Deskman never minimizes, closes or hides a window, and never moves the window you are working in.

Every action passes through one gateway, which records who asked: you, or Deskman on its own. Whatever you ask for gets done. When Deskman acts on its own:

  • never minimizes, closes, hides or goes full screen
  • never moves the window you are working in
  • never touches a meeting app's window
  • moves nothing at all while a meeting is on
  • leaves alone the apps you choose
  • never makes windows overlap, or smaller than 700 × 400

Nothing leaves your Mac

The model runs on the device, through Apple's FoundationModels framework, and Deskman makes no network calls. Your arrangements live in owner-only files in ~/Library/Application Support/Deskman. Forget My Activity erases them whenever you like.

Click Allow… in the panel and turn on Deskman under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Run Deskman or Hammerspoon's zone manager, not both, since they use the same keys. The README covers everything else, and every design decision is written down as an ADR.
